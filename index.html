Rollback Watch

Ballot measures and bills that would take legal access away from consumers, in the order they'll be decided, plus one rollback already in force. Updated as of October 3, 2026.

  1. Nov 3, 2026Statewide ballot
    On the ballot

    Massachusetts Question 8

    Massachusetts

    Would repeal the laws that let adults buy cannabis in licensed stores and grow it at home, nearly ten years after voters approved them. Adults could still possess up to an ounce, and the medical program would stay.

    Voters are against it in every recent poll. The University of New Hampshire found 71% opposed and 24% in favor. MassINC's September 14–22 poll of 800 likely voters found 61% opposed and 27% in favor.

    Sources: NORML, CommonWealth Beacon, NORML

  2. Nov 3, 2026Constitutional amendment
    On the ballot

    Idaho HJR 4

    Idaho

    Would give the Legislature sole power to legalize marijuana and other psychoactive drugs, and bar citizens from doing it by ballot initiative. Idaho has no legal cannabis program of any kind today, and this year's medical cannabis initiative fell short of the signatures it needed. If HJR 4 passes, future initiatives would be off the table entirely.

    Sources: Ballotpedia, Marijuana Moment

  3. Nov 3, 2026City ballot
    On the ballot

    Estacada Measure 3-638

    Estacada, Oregon

    Would block new cannabis production, processing and wholesale businesses in the city. Existing businesses keep operating, and stores and dispensaries can still open.

    Source: Estacada News

  4. Dec 11, 2026Federal law
    Takes effect

    Federal hemp-THC ban

    Nationwide

    Hemp products with more than 0.4mg total THC per container become illegal under federal law. A stopgap funding bill signed September 3 moved the date from November 12 to December 11. Synthetic cannabinoids the plant can't make on its own are still banned from November 12. Bills to regulate these products instead are pending in Congress.

    Sources: Marijuana Moment, Akerman LLP

  5. Jul 1, 2027State law
    Takes effect

    Virginia public-use fine increase

    Virginia

    The fine for using cannabis in public goes from $25 to $250. A second offense adds required education or treatment, and a third becomes a misdemeanor. Black Virginians are about 19% of the population but received 47.7% of public-use citations. Advocates are asking lawmakers to reverse the increase before it starts.

    Source: Marijuana Moment

  6. 2027Signature drive
    Gathering signatures

    Maine sales repeal

    Maine

    Would outlaw adult-use cannabis sales starting in 2028, end home grow and add new testing and tracking rules for medical cannabis. The campaign missed its February 2026 deadline and is now aiming for the 2027 ballot. In June, organizers said they had about 40,000 of the 67,682 signatures needed. SAM Action has put in $2 million.

    Sources: MJBizDaily, Marijuana Moment

  7. 2027 sessionLegislature
    Expected

    Oregon SB 1548, again

    Oregon

    The 10mg-per-edible cap passed the Senate 22-5 in 2026, then died in a House committee after consumers pushed back. Its sponsor, Sen. Lisa Reynolds, says she'll bring it back in 2027 with new warning labels.

    Sources: Marijuana Moment, What the bill did and why it failed

  8. In forceSince Mar 21, 2026
    Now law

    Ohio SB 56

    Ohio

    Lawmakers rewrote the adult-use law voters passed in 2023. It's now a crime to bring home cannabis bought legally in another state. Flower is capped at 35% THC and extracts at 70%, smoking is banned in most public places, and products must stay in original packaging. A referendum drive to stop it fell short. It's the model for rolling back voter-approved laws without a vote.

    Source: Marijuana Moment

The facts are on our side.

Prohibition-era talking points don't hold up. These four come up in almost every hearing.

8.7%

of teens used cannabis last year

Teen use keeps falling as legalization spreads. Past-year use among 12- to 17-year-olds dropped from 10.4% in 2024 to 8.7% in 2025.

SAMHSA, National Survey on Drug Use and Health, 2025

Not top 10

Edibles in child poisonings

In Oregon, household cleaners, cosmetics and pain relievers each caused more than 1,600 poisonings in kids under 6. Cannabis edibles didn't make the top 10.

Oregon Poison Center annual report

0

Respiratory depression from THC

THC can't shut down breathing the way opioids can. The receptors work differently. That's the finding of a peer-reviewed pharmacology review.

Wiese et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2023

88%

of kids' edible ER visits trace to storage

Most pediatric edible emergencies come down to products that weren't locked up. Storage education works. Rewrapping every gummy doesn't fix that.

Canadian review, ScienceDirect

“The real solution is educating adults to store cannabis products locked and out of reach. That's what actually changes outcomes.”Dr. Rob Hendrickson, Medical Director, Oregon Poison Center, on OPB's Think Out Loud

2026 is the year reefer madness came back.

Opponents stopped arguing about whether cannabis should be legal and started taking legalization apart piece by piece. Ohio's legislature rewrote the law voters passed. Massachusetts votes on repealing legal sales November 3. A federal ban on hemp-derived THC takes effect in December. Oregon's Senate passed a 10mg cap on every edible. And police made more cannabis arrests in 2025 than the year before.

The arguments are the old ones, and the evidence still doesn't back them. What's been missing is an organized consumer voice to say so in every session and on every ballot. ACCA is that voice.
