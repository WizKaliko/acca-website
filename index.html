Consumers deserve a seat at the table.

Cannabis policy is written by regulators, industry lobbyists and prohibition groups. The 64 million Americans who actually use cannabis rarely have anyone in the room. We're building that voice, starting in Oregon and Hawai'i and growing toward something national.

We are not the industry.We represent people who use cannabis, not the businesses that sell it. Industry can sponsor an event. It can't set our positions.

Evidence over panic.Bad policy gets made when misinformation fills the room. We bring poison center data, peer-reviewed science and federal surveys.

Organized and ready.When a bill moves against consumers, our network shows up in hearing rooms, in legislators' inboxes and at the ballot box.

Rollback Watch

Ballot measures and bills that would take legal access away from consumers, in the order they'll be decided. Updated as of October 3, 2026.

  1. Nov 3, 2026Statewide ballot
    On the ballot

    Massachusetts Question 8

    Massachusetts

    Would repeal the laws that let adults buy cannabis in licensed stores and grow it at home, nearly ten years after voters approved them.

    A University of New Hampshire poll released September 25 found 71% of voters oppose it and 24% support it.

    Sources: NORML, NORML

  2. Nov 3, 2026Constitutional amendment
    On the ballot

    Idaho HJR 4

    Idaho

    Would give the Legislature sole power to legalize marijuana and bar citizens from doing it by ballot initiative. Idaho has no legal cannabis program of any kind today.

    Source: Ballotpedia

  3. Nov 3, 2026City ballot
    On the ballot

    Estacada Measure 3-638

    Estacada, Oregon

    Would block new cannabis production, processing and wholesale businesses in the city. Existing businesses keep operating, and stores and dispensaries can still open.

    Source: Estacada News

    Maine

    Would outlaw adult-use cannabis sales starting in 2028 and add new testing and tracking rules for medical cannabis. Organizers say they have about 40,000 of the 67,682 signatures needed for the 2027 ballot. SAM Action has put in $2 million.

    Source: MJBizDaily

The facts are on our side.

Prohibition-era talking points don't hold up. These four come up in almost every hearing.

30-year

low in teen cannabis use

Past-year use among 12th graders is the lowest in three decades. In 19 of 21 states with before-and-after data, teen use went down after legalization.

NIDA, Monitoring the Future, 2024

Not top 10

Edibles in child poisonings

In Oregon, household cleaners, cosmetics and pain relievers each caused more than 1,600 poisonings in kids under 6. Cannabis edibles didn't make the top 10.

Oregon Poison Center annual report

0

Respiratory depression from THC

THC can't shut down breathing the way opioids can. The receptors work differently. That's the finding of a peer-reviewed pharmacology review.

Wiese et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2023

88%

of kids' edible ER visits trace to storage

Most pediatric edible emergencies come down to products that weren't locked up. Storage education works. Rewrapping every gummy doesn't fix that.

Canadian review, ScienceDirect

“The real solution is educating adults to store cannabis products locked and out of reach. That's what actually changes outcomes.”Dr. Rob Hendrickson, Medical Director, Oregon Poison Center, on OPB's Think Out Loud

We started because no one was in the room.

In 2025, Oregon's SB 1548 came within a few votes of becoming law. It would have forced individual wrapping on every edible and capped each piece at 10mg, based on claims that Oregon's own Poison Center data didn't support.

Consumers and businesses pushed back and the bill died. But there was no permanent consumer voice to hold that ground between sessions. ACCA was built to be that voice.
