Built by consumers. For everyone.

ACCA exists because when Oregon's SB 1548 was moving, there was no permanent, organized consumer voice in the room.

Why we exist, and why now

In 2025, Oregon Senate Bill 1548 came within a few votes of becoming law. It would have required individual wrapping for every edible piece and capped each at 10mg THC. It was framed as child safety, but Oregon's own Poison Center data, peer-reviewed pharmacology and packaging research didn't support it.

Industry groups and consumers pushed back together, and it made one thing clear: consumers need a permanent voice in every session, not just when a crisis bill is already moving.

We are not a trade association. We don't represent growers, processors or retailers. We represent the 64 million Americans who used cannabis last year and deserve policy that treats them like adults.

How we got here

  1. 1973Oregon decriminalizes

    Oregon becomes the first state to decriminalize cannabis, making possession of up to an ounce a civil fine.

  2. 1996–1998Medical legalization begins

    California legalizes medical cannabis with Prop 215. Oregon, Alaska and Washington follow by ballot in 1998. A regulated market is born with no organized consumer voice.

  3. 2012–2014Adult use arrives

    Colorado and Washington legalize in 2012. Oregon and Alaska follow in 2014; Oregon's Measure 91 passes 56% to 44%.

  4. 2016Four more states

    California, Nevada, Maine and Massachusetts legalize adult use on one election night.

  5. 2018–2021The wave builds

    Vermont becomes the first state to legalize through its legislature. Michigan, Illinois, Arizona, Montana, New Jersey, New York, Virginia and New Mexico follow.

  6. 2022–202324 states

    Rhode Island, Connecticut, Maryland, Delaware, Minnesota, Missouri and Ohio bring the total to 24 adult-use states. Consumers are still unrepresented in policy.

  7. 2025The wake-up call

    Oregon's SB 1548 moves through the Legislature. Consumers and industry push back and it dies, but there's no permanent consumer voice to hold that ground between sessions.

  8. 2026ACCA founded

    The American Cannabis Consumer Alliance launches to fill that gap, building the list, the research library and the infrastructure before the next session.

What we stand for

Six principles that decide every position we take.

Consumers first

We represent people who use cannabis, not the businesses that sell it. Our positions follow what's good for people who use cannabis legally and responsibly.

Evidence over ideology

Every position rests on peer-reviewed science, government data or primary reporting. When the facts change, we change with them.

Regulation works

The licensed market is safer for consumers, communities and public health than prohibition or the unregulated market. We defend regulation and work to improve it.

Equity is non-negotiable

Prohibition fell hardest on communities of color. A cannabis policy that doesn't address those harms is incomplete. We bring that lens to every fight.

Transparency always

We're open about who funds us, what we spend money on and what positions we hold. Consumers deserve to know who speaks in their name, and why.

Show up

Good advocacy means being present: in hearing rooms, at community meetings and in the inbox of every legislator considering a bill that affects consumers.

Founder

Kaliko Castille

Founder and Executive Director

Kaliko has spent more than a decade at the intersection of cannabis policy, community organizing and political strategy. He was Director of Marketing at the National Cannabis Industry Association and Board President of the Minority Cannabis Business Association, where he led MCBA's National Cannabis Equity Report and helped develop model legalization policy for state legislatures. His federal work included direct lobbying for equitable cannabis reform.

He is Political Director at APANO Action Fund and founded ACCA after the SB 1548 fight. His work has been featured on MSNBC and in Forbes, Bloomberg, Rolling Stone, The Washington Post and The Hill, and he was named to MJ Venture's 40 Under 40.

Our commitments to you

What you can hold us to.

No industry control

Industry can sponsor events. Industry can't set our positions. Those come from consumer interests and evidence.

Published financials

Once we're formally established, we'll publish annual financial disclosures so members know how their money is used.

Your data is yours

We'll never sell, rent or share your personal information, including with industry partners or sponsors.

Public positions

Every bill we support or oppose, every testimony and every statement we make gets posted on this site.
